# The system we are actually trying to build

I do not think the target should be “an LLM that understands aptness, fallibilism, and hermeneutics.”

That formulation has already conceded too much. It assumes the artefact before establishing the capability, and it subtly turns every concept into another behaviour the language model must imitate.

The more faithful target is:

> **A controlled system that regulates when meaning should be resolved, when a claim should be investigated, when a position should be revised, and when a coherent remainder should be preserved for the human receiver.**

Its central competence would not be answering. It would be **regulating closure**.

An answer is only one possible outcome.

---

## 1. The system would not manufacture human depth

Your aptness document makes depth relational. The compressed expression alone does not contain the depth; it activates a shared foundation and creates an opening that the receiver completes through their own history and experience. The receiver also recognises that this completion is partly theirs. 

That means no model can independently “produce depth” in the full sense described there. At most, it can produce or preserve the **conditions under which depth may arise for someone**.

This distinction should govern the entire project:

* We are not reproducing human lived interpretation.
* We are not giving the machine a synthetic worldview.
* We are not equating stochastic variation with personal experience.
* We are not asking it to feel doubt, curiosity, justice or wonder.
* We are designing explicit operations analogous to some consequences of those dispositions.

The system would not possess fallibilism as a virtue. It would maintain revisable claims.

It would not experience uncertainty. It would distinguish different forms of incompleteness.

It would not personally interpret an apt expression. It would recognise shared anchors, follow locally connected paths and avoid prematurely exhausting the expression.

It would not become a co-author in the same way a human receiver does. It would preserve a space in which the human can become one.

So this is best understood as a **depth-enabling system**, not a deep artificial person.

---

# 2. The purpose: earned closure

Most contemporary intelligent systems implicitly operate toward completion:

> input → disambiguation → inference → answer

Even when uncertainty is acknowledged, it is usually treated as an obstacle to be reduced. Your two documents jointly imply that this is not always appropriate.

The first document supplies the principle of **investigation**: a claim should remain revisable, and uncertainty may require action rather than merely a verbal disclaimer. 

The second supplies the principle of **generative remainder**: some unresolved content is not missing information but the very source of an expression’s depth. 

The system therefore needs to distinguish at least five conditions that ordinary confidence scores collapse together.

| Condition                           | What remains unresolved                                                             | Appropriate operation             |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Incoherence                         | No stable shared interpretation exists                                              | Reject, repair or request context |
| Epistemic insufficiency             | The question has an answer, but evidence is missing                                 | Investigate                       |
| Semantic ambiguity                  | Several meanings are possible because the signal is unclear                         | Disambiguate                      |
| Perspectival or normative plurality | Several positions remain legitimate under different commitments                     | Contrast and expose assumptions   |
| Generative openness                 | A shared anchor exists, but completion appropriately belongs partly to the receiver | Preserve the remainder            |

The most important component may therefore be neither the language model nor the knowledge base. It may be a **closure regulator** whose job is to identify which kind of unresolvedness is present.

The governing principle would be:

> **Closure must be earned by the character of the inquiry, not imposed by the system’s default output behaviour.**

---

# 3. We do not yet know the output, but we can know its invariants

Not knowing the final output is not necessarily a defect. It only becomes a problem when the development process is output-first.

We can begin by specifying what every valid outcome must preserve, regardless of whether it eventually appears as prose, a graph, a question, an argument map, a metaphor, a recommendation or an unfinished interpretive opening.

A valid system state should expose:

1. **What is shared:** the anchor or common ground from which reasoning began.
2. **What is inferred:** claims or interpretations added by the system.
3. **How it arrived there:** the local path connecting each movement.
4. **What supports it:** evidence, assumptions, rules or contextual grounds.
5. **What remains unresolved:** and, crucially, what kind of unresolvedness it is.
6. **Why the system stopped:** sufficient resolution, insufficient evidence, value conflict, generative openness or resource limits.
7. **What belongs to the receiver:** the part the system should not silently complete on their behalf.
8. **How revision would occur:** what evidence or changed assumption could alter the current state.

These are **output invariants**, not an output format.

The terminal result may be:

* a resolved answer;
* a provisional claim;
* an investigation request;
* a map of competing interpretations;
* a suspended judgment;
* an apt expression with a deliberately retained remainder;
* or a structured invitation for the human to continue.

The system should be allowed to end in different epistemic states rather than dressing every state as a conventional answer.

# 4. Operationalization without reducing philosophy to metrics

The philosophical ideas should not be operationalized by searching immediately for a numerical score corresponding to each concept.

A more defensible translation has four parts:

> **invariants, admissible operations, prohibited transformations and observable traces**

For example:

| Concept                  | Invariant                                                            | Operation                                | Prohibited transformation                                          | Observable trace                                  |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Fallibilism              | Claims remain conditional on grounds and assumptions                 | Challenge, revise, retract               | Presenting generated plausibility as settled knowledge             | Revision and dependency history                   |
| Investigation            | Relevant uncertainty can cause evidence-seeking action               | Retrieve, test, compare, ask             | Merely saying “I may be wrong”                                     | Record of what was checked and why                |
| Aptness                  | Shared coherence coexists with an unexhausted remainder              | Compress, suggest, preserve              | Either complete paraphrastic exhaustion or arbitrary vagueness     | Shared anchor plus legitimate interpretive spread |
| Hermeneutics             | Interpretation is conditioned by context and horizon                 | Recontextualize, compare horizons        | Treating meaning as entirely context-free                          | Context-sensitive interpretation history          |
| Reflexive awareness      | System contribution and receiver contribution remain distinguishable | Mark boundaries, invite completion       | Passing a receiver-dependent construction off as objective content | Explicit ownership of interpretive additions      |
| Rule-following awareness | Examples do not uniquely determine every future application          | Expose assumptions, test counterexamples | Treating dataset regularity as self-justifying norm                | Alternative rules and failure cases               |

This avoids claiming that we have captured the philosophical essence of fallibilism or hermeneutics.

We are instead asking:

> What organisation of a computational system would be incompatible with violating the idea in obvious ways?

That is a more modest and tractable form of operationalization.

# 5. The basic ontology

The system should not represent everything as undifferentiated text. It needs typed objects because different forms of meaning have different validity conditions.

A factual claim can be true or false.

An interpretation may be apt, strained or contextually ungrounded.

A value judgment may be consistent or inconsistent with declared commitments without being empirically falsifiable.

A metaphor may be illuminating without being literally true.

A question may be valuable because it reorganises an inquiry rather than because it asserts anything.

The initial ontology could include the following.

### Inquiry

The object currently being explored:

* goal;
* domain;
* context;
* stakes;
* requested action;
* closure policy;
* available resources.

### Anchor

Something that constrains the inquiry:

* shared linguistic convention;
* observed fact;
* source;
* domain rule;
* user-supplied experience;
* declared value;
* prior accepted claim.

### Claim

A proposition with:

* grounds;
* source;
* assumptions;
* scope;
* dependencies;
* counterevidence;
* uncertainty;
* revision history.

### Interpretation

A possible understanding whose quality depends on:

* fidelity to the source signal;
* contextual fit;
* relational continuity;
* explanatory power;
* horizon or perspective;
* alternatives.

### Path

A sequence of locally connected transitions through claims or interpretations.

### Tension

A relation that cannot currently be harmonised:

* contradiction;
* competing value;
* incompatible interpretation;
* unresolved assumption;
* evidence conflict.

### Remainder

Content intentionally or unintentionally left unresolved, typed as:

* evidence gap;
* ambiguity;
* pluralism;
* open question;
* generative remainder.

### Decision state

The system’s current position:

* resolve;
* continue;
* investigate;
* contrast;
* suspend;
* rollback;
* preserve openness;
* transfer to human judgment.

The system’s primary internal product would be this evolving inquiry state. Natural-language output would be a rendering of it, not the state itself.

# 6. The architecture

A provisional architecture would have five interacting planes.

```text
                         ┌────────────────────────┐
User / environment ────►│  Interaction interface │
                         └────────────┬───────────┘
                                      │
                         ┌────────────▼───────────┐
                         │    Inquiry compiler     │
                         └────────────┬───────────┘
                                      │
                  ┌───────────────────▼───────────────────┐
                  │       Shared inquiry state            │
                  │ anchors • claims • paths • tensions   │
                  │ evidence • horizons • remainders      │
                  └───────┬──────────┬──────────┬─────────┘
                          │          │          │
              ┌───────────▼───┐ ┌────▼─────┐ ┌──▼────────────┐
              │ Interpretive   │ │Epistemic │ │Domain-anchor │
              │ path engine    │ │ledger    │ │and tool layer│
              └───────────┬───┘ └────┬─────┘ └──┬────────────┘
                          │          │          │
                          └──────────┼──────────┘
                                     │
                         ┌───────────▼────────────┐
                         │   Closure regulator     │
                         └───────────┬────────────┘
                                     │
                         ┌───────────▼────────────┐
                         │ Expression / action     │
                         │ composer                │
                         └─────────────────────────┘
```

## 6.1 The inquiry compiler

This converts an input into a typed inquiry rather than immediately asking a generator for an answer.

It determines:

* Is the user asking for a fact, interpretation, judgment, decision, exploration or creation?
* What kind of closure is requested?
* What would count as a serious error?
* Which parts are explicit and which have been assumed?
* Does the signal appear deliberately compressed?
* Is there an evident speech act beyond the literal wording?

An LLM could assist here, but its output should be treated as a proposed parse rather than authoritative intent detection.

## 6.2 The epistemic ledger

This is the fallibilist core.

It stores claims together with their dependencies and assumptions. An assumption-based truth-maintenance system is a useful historical analogue: such systems organise conclusions according to the assumption sets under which they hold, allowing conclusions to be withdrawn or recomputed when assumptions change. ([ScienceDirect][1])

Argumentation frameworks offer another useful component. Instead of immediately deciding which proposition is true, they can represent attacks and support relations among arguments and calculate different acceptable sets under different semantics. ([ScienceDirect][2])

Neither framework captures aptness, but together they provide something current generative systems often lack: a persistent distinction between:

* a claim;
* the reasons for it;
* the assumptions permitting it;
* and competing claims that remain live.

## 6.3 The interpretive path engine

This implements the document’s “walking, not searching” correction. It should not enumerate every possible interpretation and globally rank them. It should move locally from the last coherent state. 

A cycle might be:

1. Begin at one or more shared anchors.
2. Generate a small set of locally adjacent movements.
3. test each movement against the current connective thread;
4. accept one or retain a genuine local fork;
5. continue while the connection holds;
6. rollback to the last stable state when it fails;
7. stop when further movement would become arbitrary, redundant or improperly exhaustive.

The candidate generator may be neural, symbolic, retrieval-based or hybrid. The important part is that it cannot silently replace the current path with a globally plausible but locally ungrounded association.

### The connective thread

We do not yet possess a complete definition of this, but we can avoid collapsing it into one similarity score.

A transition could be evaluated through a profile containing:

* referential continuity;
* relational or structural continuity;
* pragmatic fit;
* causal or argumentative continuity;
* thematic continuity;
* contextual compatibility;
* affective or tonal continuity where relevant;
* counterfactual dependence on the original cue;
* novelty;
* non-arbitrariness.

Some dimensions may function as vetoes rather than weighted preferences. A continuation could be semantically similar but pragmatically irrelevant. It could be novel but disconnected. It could be coherent but merely restate what is already explicit.

The system should preserve this vector rather than summing it immediately into “goodness.”

A particularly valuable test is **cue dependence**:

> Would this continuation still appear equally appropriate if the original cue were replaced by a different one?

When the answer is yes, the continuation may be generic depth theatre rather than an interpretation genuinely generated through the signal.

## 6.4 The domain-anchor layer

Different inquiries require different forms of answerability.

For factual inquiry, anchors may include observation, provenance, reproducibility and source agreement.

For historical interpretation, they may include primary evidence, temporal consistency, contextual fidelity and separation between record and inference.

For normative inquiry, they may include affected parties, declared values, reversibility, consistency across analogous cases, consequences and the legitimacy of the procedure.

For textual or artistic interpretation, they may include fidelity to the work, explanatory reach, internal coherence, historical context and the reader’s acknowledged horizon.

For creative aptness, they may include shared convergence, productive interpretive spread, local traceability and resistance to complete paraphrase.

This is not one universal theory of truth. It is a registry of **domain-specific anchors and permissible investigative operations**.

## 6.5 The closure regulator

This is the architectural centre.

It receives the current inquiry state and decides among operations such as:

* continue the interpretive path;
* gather evidence;
* expose an assumption;
* compare perspectives;
* ask the user;
* rollback;
* conclude provisionally;
* preserve the remainder;
* or stop because further processing would add only decorative verbosity.

It should consider:

* the kind of unresolvedness;
* stakes;
* expected value of further investigation;
* domain requirements;
* user purpose;
* reversibility of error;
* cost of continued inquiry;
* danger of destroying productive openness.

This makes investigation conditional rather than compulsive. Some active-inference formulations explicitly combine goal-directed value with epistemic, information-seeking value, providing a formal precedent for choosing actions partly because they reduce uncertainty. ([MIT Press Direct][3])

But our system cannot simply minimize uncertainty. In apt interpretation, complete uncertainty reduction may destroy the desired result. The regulator therefore needs a distinction between **harmful uncertainty** and **productive openness**.

## 6.6 The expression layer

Only after the inquiry state is evaluated should the system decide how to communicate it.

The renderer might produce:

* direct prose;
* a compact answer;
* an evidence table;
* an argument map;
* several situated readings;
* a question;
* a deliberately compressed expression;
* or an interactive sequence that lets the receiver complete the final movement.

An LLM may be particularly useful here as a linguistic codec. But it would express a prior state rather than inventing the system’s position entirely during token generation.

# 7. Aptness as metastability rather than completion

Associative-memory research offers a potentially useful mathematical vocabulary.

Modern Hopfield networks have been described as possessing several kinds of attractor states: convergence toward one stored pattern, averaging across a subset of patterns, or more global averaging. The corresponding update rule has also been shown to be equivalent to transformer attention under the paper’s formulation. ([arXiv][4])

This does not solve aptness, but it suggests a productive analogy.

Current inference is often treated as descent toward a stable answer-attractor. Aptness may instead correspond to a **controlled metastable region**:

* constrained enough not to dissolve into arbitrary association;
* stable enough that readers recognise the same shared opening;
* but not collapsed into one fully specified representation.

“The city breathed” may activate a basin around life, rhythm and organic movement without requiring every receiver to converge onto the same concrete scene.

The technical target would not necessarily be to eliminate attractors. It might be to control **how far the state is allowed to settle**.

That gives us a possible interpretation of the coherence threshold:

> Coherence is the existence of a shared basin. Depth is the preservation of meaningful internal degrees of freedom within that basin.

This remains speculative, but it is more precise than trying to reward outputs that merely “feel profound.”

# 8. Compression must preserve generativity, not only relevance

The information bottleneck formalises the search for a compressed representation that preserves information relevant to another variable or task. ([arXiv][5])

Aptness asks for a different kind of compression.

Conventional task compression asks:

> What minimum representation preserves what is needed to predict the target?

Apt compression asks something closer to:

> What minimum signal preserves the shared relational structure while leaving the receiver enough coherent freedom to generate situated meaning?

The retained information is therefore not only task relevance. It includes **generative potential**.

A compressed signal that predicts one canonical interpretation perfectly may be efficient but not apt. An apt signal preserves a region of legitimate continuations.

This suggests that aptness cannot be evaluated solely by reconstruction accuracy. We need to study the geometry of what remains possible after compression:

* Do receivers converge initially?
* Do they diverge later?
* Is the divergence conditioned by their context?
* Can each path be traced back to the cue?
* Does replacing the cue change the paths?
* Do receivers recognise their own contribution?
* Does explicit expansion destroy something the compressed signal achieved?

The result should be an **aptness profile**, not a single scalar reward.

# 9. Related technical domains and their limited contributions

No existing paradigm supplies the entire system, but several provide useful partial mechanisms.

### Rational pragmatics

Rational Speech Act models formally represent communication as recursive reasoning among speakers and listeners, offering implementable models of pragmatic inference beyond literal meaning. ([arXiv][6])

They may help with Layer 1—shared, convergent repair—but they generally do not capture the full personally constituted remainder described in the document.

### Semantic uncertainty

Semantic-entropy work clusters differently worded generations according to whether they express the same meaning, distinguishing verbal diversity from uncertainty over meanings. ([Nature][7])

That can help determine whether a system is merely paraphrasing or entertaining substantively different claims. But legitimate interpretive plurality must not automatically be treated as hallucination or epistemic failure.

### Latent or concept-level prediction

Large Concept Models explore prediction in sentence-level representation spaces rather than exclusively at the token level. ([arXiv][8]) Joint-embedding predictive proposals likewise explore predicting representations rather than reconstructing all raw detail. ([OpenReview][9])

These show that token-level autoregression is not the only possible representational finish line. Yet changing the unit from token to sentence or latent vector does not by itself create aptness, fallibilism or closure regulation.

### Iterative generation

Diffusion-style language systems make revision across multiple positions structurally easier than irrevocable left-to-right generation, although recent work also shows that iterative refinement and reliable correction do not arise automatically from the diffusion objective. ([arXiv][10])

This could be useful for revision, but the system still needs an external account of what should be revised and what should remain open.

### Associative and energy-based systems

Associative memories may provide the terrain for local walks, attractor regions and controlled return to stable states. They are promising as a substrate, but an energy minimum cannot be assumed to correspond to truth, aptness or justified closure.

### Symbolic and argument-based systems

Truth maintenance, non-monotonic reasoning and argumentation provide explicit revision and conflict representation. Their weakness is that they require prior formalisation and may strip away the very richness we want to preserve.

The likely answer is therefore not one of these approaches. It is a **heterogeneous architecture** in which each mechanism is used only for the operation it can genuinely support.

# 10. A development programme

We should resist designing a novel neural architecture first. The conceptual distinctions need to survive small experiments before they become training objectives.

## Phase 0: Write the constitutional research constraints

Before implementation, establish what the project must not silently become.

Examples:

* It must not equate openness with randomness.
* It must not equate verbosity with depth.
* It must not model users as if inferred profiles were lived experience.
* It must not reduce every domain to factual verification.
* It must not reward one canonical interpretation where plurality is legitimate.
* It must not preserve ambiguity when evidence could resolve a consequential question.
* It must not make the LLM’s generated trace the authoritative epistemic state.
* It must not compress all qualities into one reward score.

These constraints should remain versioned and testable.

## Phase 1: Build the phenomenon dataset

Create a deliberately small, high-quality experimental corpus rather than a massive training dataset.

It should contain matched cases such as:

* apt versus flat;
* apt versus incoherent;
* ambiguity versus openness;
* evidence gap versus value disagreement;
* meaningful compression versus generic vagueness;
* legitimate plurality versus factual contradiction;
* locally connected continuation versus thematic drift.

For each item, collect human responses in stages:

1. immediate shared interpretation;
2. next associative step;
3. later personal interpretation;
4. where the connection stopped holding;
5. whether they considered their completion part of the expression or their own contribution;
6. whether an expanded paraphrase retained or destroyed the effect.

This dataset is not initially for model training. It is for determining whether the distinctions can be observed reliably at all.

## Phase 2: Build the aptness probe

The first system should not generate apt language. It should analyse candidate expressions.

Its output would be an aptness profile:

* strength of shared anchor;
* early convergence;
* later interpretive spread;
* path traceability;
* dependence on receiver context;
* coherence threshold;
* resistance to exhaustive paraphrase;
* reflexive recognition;
* likelihood of generic profundity.

This lets us test whether the concept is empirically distinguishable before attempting to produce it.

## Phase 3: Build the local-walk engine

Use an explicit graph or memory structure.

A practical research prototype could use:

* embeddings for local semantic neighbourhoods;
* a lightweight concept and relation graph;
* contextual constraints;
* a candidate generator;
* a multidimensional connective-thread evaluator;
* rollback to the last stable node;
* path event logging.

The graph should initially remain inspectable. A sophisticated learned latent terrain can come later.

The main experiment is not “Can it write beautifully?” It is:

> Can it move beyond the obvious shared anchor without either collapsing into repetition or drifting into unrelated association?

## Phase 4: Add the epistemic ledger

Represent claims, evidence, assumptions and conflicts separately from interpretations.

The ledger should support:

* dependency-aware revision;
* contradictory evidence;
* alternative assumption sets;
* provenance;
* domain-specific validity procedures;
* suspended claims;
* explicit distinction between observation and inference.

At this point the system can begin handling factual investigation and interpretive inquiry within the same overall structure without treating them as identical.

## Phase 5: Implement the closure regulator

Initially, this should be rule-based and inspectable.

It will classify the inquiry state and choose an operation:

```text
incoherence             → repair or stop
missing evidence         → investigate
ordinary ambiguity       → disambiguate
competing value systems  → contrast
stable factual support   → resolve provisionally
coherent openness        → preserve remainder
path drift               → rollback
human-owned judgment     → transfer
```

Only after these decisions can be evaluated should we consider learning the control policy.

## Phase 6: Add language models as bounded components

An existing language model could perform:

* input parsing;
* candidate interpretation generation;
* extraction of claims from sources;
* paraphrasing;
* hypothesis generation;
* final-language rendering.

But each generated item enters the explicit state as a candidate with provenance:

> `origin = generative proposal`

It does not become accepted because it is fluent.

This phase tests the broader system without committing to LLMs as its cognitive core.

## Phase 7: Human interaction experiments

Aptness is relational, so evaluation cannot be completed entirely offline.

The system should be tested with different receivers while controlling:

* common knowledge;
* domain familiarity;
* explicitly supplied context;
* cultural and linguistic background where ethically and practically appropriate;
* prior exposure to the expression.

The question is not simply whether people “like” the output. It is whether:

* they converge on an identifiable shared foundation;
* their later interpretations vary coherently;
* their variations can be explained through their supplied context;
* they recognise the remaining contribution as their own;
* and the system avoids manipulating them into believing that arbitrary vagueness is profound.

## Phase 8: Only then consider a new model architecture

If the explicit prototype shows that the target phenomenon is real and measurable, we can ask whether the scaffolding should be consolidated into a learned architecture.

Possible directions would include:

* learned associative terrains;
* metastable energy-based representations;
* concept-level rather than token-level state transitions;
* graph–neural hybrids;
* probabilistic interpretation fields;
* non-monotonic neural memory;
* iterative latent refinement;
* separate shared and situated representation layers;
* a learned controller trained on operation selection rather than answer preference.

At that stage, “LLM or not?” becomes an empirical question rather than a founding assumption.

# 11. A practical Version 0.1

The first integrated prototype could accept:

```text
signal or question
explicit context
domain
desired purpose
stakes
```

It would produce an **Inquiry Packet** containing:

```text
1. Inquiry type
2. Shared anchors
3. Current claims
4. Interpretive path
5. Evidence and assumptions
6. Tensions or counterpositions
7. Type of unresolvedness
8. Closure decision
9. Generative remainder, when present
10. Human-facing rendering
```

Consider the word **justice**.

The system might establish a shared anchor around fairness, legitimacy, treatment and social order. It may then follow one locally grounded path through virtue, institutions, equality, desert or repair. At each movement it records what enabled the transition.

It should not pretend that this path exhausts justice.

It may discover that a factual claim within the discussion requires evidence, while the larger disagreement depends on incompatible views about equality and responsibility. The closure regulator would investigate the former and preserve or contrast the latter.

For **“The city breathed,”** it might identify the shared anchor around organic life and rhythm, show several locally grounded possibilities, and then stop before replacing the expression with a definitive interpretation.

For **“The city was big,”** it would recognise that ordinary inference resolves the meaning with little productive remainder.

For **“Colorless green ideas sleep furiously,”** it might initially detect failure of shared repair—but context could alter that judgment. In a discussion of syntax, surrealism or language philosophy, the sentence has a clear function even though its literal scene resists coherent construction. The system must therefore assess not only sentence semantics but the larger speech act.

This is exactly why no isolated “aptness classifier” will be sufficient.

---

# 12. The principal dangers

The project will fail if it produces elegant language while leaving the underlying state unchanged. That would be **depth theatre**.

It will also fail if it:

* invents a user’s personal horizon from stereotypes;
* retains uncertainty to appear sophisticated;
* refuses to answer questions that should be answered;
* describes every issue as perspectival;
* treats factual falsehoods as alternative interpretations;
* formalises aptness so aggressively that apt expression becomes impossible;
* rewards divergence until coherence disappears;
* rewards convergence until generative remainder disappears;
* turns the explicit inquiry state into hidden chain-of-thought prose rather than an auditable structure;
* or gradually lets the LLM become the real controller while the architecture survives only as decoration.

The strongest safeguard is separation of powers:

* generation proposes;
* evidence constrains;
* domain anchors evaluate;
* the ledger remembers;
* the closure regulator decides;
* and the human retains the parts of meaning that properly belong to human completion.

---

# 13. The working definition

The system can provisionally be defined as:

> **A heterogeneous, controlled inquiry architecture that maintains typed and revisable epistemic and interpretive states, follows locally grounded meaning paths, distinguishes different forms of unresolvedness, and regulates closure so that factual uncertainty is investigated while coherent generative openness can remain uncollapsed.**

Its deepest commitment is not to any model family.

It is this:

> **Intelligence is not the maximal elimination of uncertainty. It is the disciplined determination of what should be resolved, what should be revised, what should be investigated, and what should remain open.**

The next concrete step is to turn this rumination into a **research charter and Version 0.1 specification**: the ontology, closure-state taxonomy, system contracts, initial experimental corpus and success/failure tests—without yet committing to a novel neural architecture.
